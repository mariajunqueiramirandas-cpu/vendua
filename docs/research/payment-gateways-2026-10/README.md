# Keep Mercado Pago, then add PagBank next

Venduá should keep Mercado Pago as the anchor on both sides of its business, and treat everything else as a complement chosen by integration model before price. For **its own plan billing**, Mercado Pago preapproval stays the card rail. The one open question is whether Venduá's own MP account can collect the monthly Pix through **Pix Automático**. If it can't, **Asaas** is the best complement: Pix Automático at no surcharge over its R$ 1,99 Pix, NFS-e issued with each charge for R$ 0,49 and no monthly fee. Efí (R$ 3,50 per Pix Automático) and the plan-fee platforms Iugu and Vindi rank below it. Stripe and Pagar.me don't fit, because neither offers Pix Automático to merchants. For **merchant storefronts**, **PagBank is the best-documented second option with an OAuth "connect your own account" flow**, and a follow-up pass found **SumUp** as the other open one-click candidate. Both need a Pix test before any build. **InfinitePay** comes third, with 0% Pix and wide use among small merchants but no OAuth, only a merchant-typed handle. **Pagar.me/Stone** comes fourth, as the online gateway other builders already integrate. Asaas, Efí, and the split features of PagBank and Pagar.me are a poor fit for storefronts: they make Venduá the KYC party, the party liable for chargebacks, or both. That is the opposite of the no-custody design that 2025–26 BCB rules favour. On fees, Pix is nearly a wash (about R$ 0,00–0,71 on a R$ 60 order everywhere except Asaas's flat fee). The real spread is on cards. Mercado Pago's roughly 5% for immediate settlement is among the most expensive, against about 3,2% at Stone/Ton and 2,99% + R$ 0,49 at Asaas. One caveat applies to every number in this report. **Mercado Pago's official fee pages returned HTTP 403, so none of its rates came from an official page**, and many other providers' figures conflict across sources. Treat every rate below as a planning figure to confirm in the provider's dashboard or a written quote. Don't use any of them in a decision or in public copy until then.

## Platform billing hinges on Pix Automático, not on card fees

Venduá's current setup already covers the card side. Mercado Pago subscriptions are created through `/preapproval` with a `card_token_id` and can be repriced with `PUT /preapproval/{id}` ([Mercado Pago developers](https://www.mercadopago.com.br/developers/pt/docs/subscriptions/subscription-management)). The weak link is the **monthly Pix charge**: the merchant must actively pay each month. That is exactly what Pix Automático, live since **16 June 2025**, was built to remove. The payer authorizes once, gets notified 2–10 days before each debit, and can revoke in their bank app at any time ([Efí technical guide](https://sejaefi.com.br/blog/pix-automatico-guia-tecnico)). Payers may also cancel an individual debit until 23:59 the day before ([PagBank blog](https://blog.pagbank.com.br/pix-automatico-pagbank)). Two rules shape the choice. **Payer-side support is mandatory, but receiver-side support is optional for PSPs**, so each gateway must be checked. And PSPs may charge the receiver, while charging the payer is prohibited ([Finsiders on BCB rules](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/regulamentacao/bc-publica-novas-regras-para-pix-automatico/)). Adoption is real but still early: about **R$ 1,2 billion and ~2,8 million payments in May 2026**, up from R$ 569 million in January ([Let's Money](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/noticias/pix-automatico-1-ano-1-bilhao-mes/)). Finsiders' one-year verdict was that it "ainda não pegou", citing explicit opt-in, dependence on account balance and consumer trust ([Finsiders](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/pagamentos/pix/um-ano-depois-pix-automatico-ainda-nao-pegou-e-ha-um-motivo)). The only reported SaaS outcome is a single anecdote: one clinic SaaS cut delinquency from 11% to 4% after moving to Pix Automático ([SystemForge](https://forjadesistemas.com.br/blog/pix-automatico-recorrencia-saas-proprio-2026/)). It suggests the upside without proving it.

**Mercado Pago remains first because the cheapest path is to stay put**, if MP supports Pix Automático for Venduá's use. The evidence is ambiguous. MP markets Pix Automático for subscriptions ([MP blog](https://www.mercadopago.com.br/blog/pix-automatico-gestao-assinaturas-receita-recorrente)). A summary of MP's docs says plan-based subscription checkout accepts card or Pix Automático ([MP developers](https://www.mercadopago.com.br/developers/pt/docs/subscriptions/integration-configuration/subscription-associated-plan)). But a low-reliability developer issue reports that `/preapproval` routes to a hosted checkout, and that enabling a receiver API for Pix Automático needs MP support ([GitHub issue #603](https://github.com/Georlan/sistema-gourmet-bistro/issues/603)). On price, MP's online Pix is **0,99%**, about R$ 0,40 on the R$ 39,90 Basic plan and R$ 0,98 on PRO+ ([Marreira Digital](https://marreiradigital.com.br/calculadora-mercadopago/); [Negócios Financeiros](https://www.negociosfinanceiros.com/taxas-mercado-pago-checkout-transparente-como-funcionam/)). Both are secondary sources. The card rate is murkier. Immediate settlement is 4,98–4,99% in both secondary sources, but the 30-day rate is reported as 3,98–3,99% ([Marreira Digital](https://marreiradigital.com.br/calculadora-mercadopago/)) and as **3,03%** in other search results ([MP blog via search](https://www.mercadopago.com.br/blog/quanto-custa-receber-pagamentos-via-pix-e-codigo-qr)). On a Basic charge that is R$ 1,21 to R$ 1,99 depending on which is true and which settlement Venduá picked. The fastest way to settle this is not more research: open Venduá's own MP account and read the rate it actually pays on subscriptions.

**Asaas is the best complement if MP can't do Pix Automático, and the only one that also solves invoicing.** Asaas's official pricing page lists no monthly or setup fee. Pix costs **R$ 1,99** (R$ 0,99 promotional for the first three months), credit cards **2,99% + R$ 0,49** and boleto R$ 1,99 ([Asaas preços](https://www.asaas.com/precos-e-taxas)). It issues **NFS-e for R$ 0,49 per note**, with the charge or on a schedule for recurring charges, and integrates with NFS-e Nacional ([Asaas nota fiscal](https://www.asaas.com/nota-fiscal)). Pix Automático is exposed in the API through `paymentCreationMode: SUBSCRIPTION` ([Asaas API changelog](https://docs.asaas.com/changelog/pix-autom%C3%A1tico-cobran%C3%A7as-recorrentes-automatizadas)). Asaas says it costs the same as a normal Pix, but that claim comes from a search snippet of a blog page that returned 403 ([Asaas blog](https://blog.asaas.com/release/pix-automatico/)). Eligibility requires a CNPJ active for at least six months and an account with no DICT fraud flags. The weakness is arithmetic: a flat R$ 1,99 is **5,0% of a Basic charge** against MP's ~1,0%. That is about R$ 1,60 more per Basic subscriber per month, or R$ 160/month per 100 Pix-paying Basic merchants, before the R$ 0,49 NFS-e. The premium is worth paying only if Pix Automático measurably lifts collection, or if Venduá would otherwise have to build or buy NFS-e issuance. Among billing gateways, only Asaas, plus Superlógica, was confirmed to issue NFS-e at all ([Mind Group](https://mindconsulting.com.br/2026/07/gateways-pagamento-online-brasil-comparativo-2026/); [Superlógica help](https://assinaturas.superlogica.com/hc/pt-br/articles/360008826593-Como-emitir-nota-fiscal-automaticamente-na-gera%C3%A7%C3%A3o-da-cobran%C3%A7a)). Two discrepancies need a direct answer from Asaas. A third-party comparison lists Asaas Pix at R$ 0,49 ([Mind Group](https://mindconsulting.com.br/2026/07/gateways-pagamento-online-brasil-comparativo-2026/)). A snippet says the first 100 monthly Pix by key or static QR are free, which probably doesn't cover Pix Automático charges.

The rest of the field ranks lower for clear reasons. **Efí** has an official, documented Pix Automático API, but charges **R$ 3,50 per settled Pix Automático**, 8,8% of a Basic charge. Its ordinary Pix cobrança at 1,19% is competitive ([Efí tarifas](https://sejaefi.com.br/tarifas)). **Inter** documents a Pix Automático API, but no fee was found ([Inter developers](https://developers.inter.co/references/pix-automatico)). **Stripe Brasil** offers Pix only by invitation, after 60 days of processing history, with Pix Automático "not available" ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/how-to-enable-pix-as-a-payment-method-in-brazil)). Its cards cost 3,99% + R$ 0,39 plus 0,7% for Billing ([Stripe BR pricing](https://stripe.com/br/pricing)), about R$ 2,26 per Basic charge. **Pagar.me** documents card, boleto and debit recurrence but no merchant Pix Automático as of July 2026, per a competitor's comparison ([Chargefy](https://chargefy.io/alternativas/pagarme)). **Vindi** has the best dunning tooling (card renewal, smart retries) but starts at R$ 299/month, and API access begins at its R$ 499 Pro plan ([Vindi preços](https://vindi.com.br/precos/)). **Iugu** reportedly starts at R$ 149/month ([iugu planos](https://iugu.com/plano_conhecaaiugu), search summary). That fee alone equals the revenue of about 3,7 Basic subscribers.

The tax reform adds one forward-looking criterion. Venduá billing merchants is a B2B sale. The first phase of IBS/CBS **split payment, expected in the second half of 2027, is B2B-only and covers Pix (including Pix Automático), boleto and TED**, not cards ([Contábeis](https://www.contabeis.com.br/noticias/79382/split-payment-nao-atingira-vendas-a-pessoas-fisicas-e-cartoes-na-primeira-fase/)). Adoption is voluntary in 2027, with B2B made mandatory from 2028 ([Algoritimado](https://algoritimado.com/blogs/noticias/split-payment-2027-implementacao-gradual-art-35-rad-2026-08-31)). BCB's schedule puts dynamic QR and Pix Automático in assisted production from June 2027 ([BCB Fórum Pix](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/estabilidadefinanceira/pix/Forum_Pix_Plenaria/20260326-Forum_Pix.pdf)). Whichever PSP collects Venduá's Pix should commit in writing to supporting the split tributário APIs. That favours large, authorized institutions such as MP and Asaas over small processors.

## Storefront checkout: PagBank is the best-documented match for the connect-your-own-account model

Venduá's storefront constraints rule out most of the market before price comes up. The merchant connects their own account, money settles directly to them, Venduá never holds funds, and the card goes through a hosted redirect. Mercado Pago already does this and stays first. It is the fifth-largest acquirer by card volume (9% in January 2026) ([Let's Money](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/noticias/rede-itau-lidera-volume-adquirentes/)). It is also reported to lead Stone and PagBank in acquiring profit ([NeoFeed](https://neofeed.com.br/negocios/mercado-pago-avanca-e-ja-supera-lucro-de-stone-e-pagbank-em-adquirencia-diz-itau-bba/)), so its prices are unlikely to come under pressure. Its Pix is 0,99% with instant availability. Its card is the expensive part at about 4,98% for immediate settlement, 4,49% at 14 days and 3,98–3,99% at 30 days ([Marreira Digital](https://marreiradigital.com.br/calculadora-mercadopago/)). Venduá's merchant onboarding should therefore point out the settlement-term choice. For a merchant who doesn't need same-day card money, it is a free one-point saving.

**PagBank is the strongest second integration because it has a real, public OAuth 2.0 authorization-code flow and a large merchant base.** The follow-up pass below found one other open one-click option, SumUp, and several partner-gated ones. The merchant is redirected to `connect.pagseguro.uol.com.br/oauth2/authorize`, and scopes include `payments.create`, `payments.refund`, `checkout.create` and `accounts.read` ([PagBank Connect](https://developer.pagbank.com.br/reference/solicitar-autorizacao-via-connect-authorization)). Its hosted checkout and payment link accept Pix, card, boleto, "Pagar com PagBank", Apple Pay and Google Pay, with webhooks, a sandbox and a homologation step before production ([PagBank Checkout](https://developer.pagbank.com.br/docs/checkout)). It also has reach: 17% of card volume and about 26% of acquiring users ([Let's Money](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/noticias/rede-itau-lidera-volume-adquirentes/)). The catch is in PagBank's Split product. The platform becomes the "Primary", pays all PagSeguro fees and "will assume any chargebacks" ([PagBank Split](https://developer.pagbank.com.br/v1/docs/split-introducao)). That is incompatible with Venduá's model, so the integration should use Connect with the merchant's own credentials and no Split. Venduá's application fee is zero today anyway. Two things must be confirmed with PagBank before building. First, no Pix-specific scope appears in the docs, so it is unproven that a Connect token can create Pix charges. Second, no documented way exists to take a platform fee outside Split. PagBank's official link pricing is **Pix 0,99%** (0% promotional for new merchants for 30 days or R$ 5.000 in volume) and **card 4,20% on the 5-day plan**, or 3,97% at 14 days. Installments cost 8,52% at 2x and 21,20% at 12x, and the link doesn't accept debit ([PagBank FAQ](https://faq.pagbank.com.br/duvida/quais-sao-as-taxas-para-vender-com-link-de-pagamento/1929)). A secondary source instead quotes 4,99% + R$ 0,40 for the link ([Máquina de Cartão Boa](https://maquinadecartaoboa.com/link-de-pagamento-pagseguro/)).

**InfinitePay is third: the cheapest Pix and a likely match for food merchants, but a weaker integration.** Its Checkout API takes the merchant's `handle` (InfiniteTag) and items priced in cents. It returns a hosted link supporting Pix and card up to 12x, with `webhook_url`, `redirect_url`, `order_nsu` and a `payment_check` endpoint ([InfinitePay Checkout docs](https://www.infinitepay.io/checkout-documentacao)). There is no OAuth, no platform fee and no split, and the fetched docs show no refund or sandbox documentation. The integration risk is a mistyped or wrong handle that sends a shopper's money to someone else. No check Venduá can run alone proves ownership: a test payment shows the payer the same receipt, so it catches typos but not someone else's handle. An authenticated binding from InfinitePay is needed first. InfinitePay's own pricing page advertises **0% Pix** and card à vista at 3,15% with next-day settlement, falling to 2,69% above R$ 80k a month ([InfinitePay taxas](https://www.infinitepay.io/taxas)). A comparison site lists the payment link at 4,20% instead ([iDinheiro](https://www.idinheiro.com.br/negocios/melhores-links-de-pagamento/)), so the official figures may be for card machines. InfinitePay is CNPJ-only and positions itself as a leader in the small-business card-machine market ([InfinitePay blog](https://www.infinitepay.io/blog/melhor-maquininha-de-cartao)), a biased source. Many of Venduá's food merchants plausibly already have an account there.

**Pagar.me (Stone) is fourth: the Stone group's online gateway, and one other builders already integrate.** It should not be counted as reaching Stone's 3,7 million or Ton's 2 million business clients ([Dinheiro da Minha Empresa](https://dinheirodaminhaempresa.com/comparativos/maquininha-stone-pagbank-cielo-mercadopago-rede-2026/)): those are separate brands and accounts, and no source shows that an existing Stone or Ton account works with Pagar.me without a new Pagar.me sign-up. Confirm that with Stone before treating Pagar.me as a route to that base. Nuvemshop and Yampi integrate Pagar.me with the merchant setting it up from their own account ([Pagar.me Nuvemshop](https://docs.pagar.me/v2/docs/nuvemshop)). A claimed Pagar.me OAuth flow could not be verified and is probably false. A no-split integration with the merchant's own keys is the likely pattern, but it is not documented. Pagar.me's split, like PagBank's, requires the platform to register recipients and name a party liable for chargebacks ([Pagar.me split rules](https://docs.pagar.me/v1/docs/split-rules)), so Venduá should stay away from it. Its rates are a three-way conflict: 0,99% Pix and 4,19% card at D+1 on its own landing page ([pagar.me/precos](https://pagar.me/precos)), 3,19% card in a July 2026 comparison ([Mind Group](https://mindconsulting.com.br/2026/07/gateways-pagamento-online-brasil-comparativo-2026/)), and 1,19% + R$ 0,99 Pix with 4,39% + R$ 0,99 card from a competitor ([Chargefy](https://chargefy.io/alternativas/pagarme)).

Several providers look attractive on price but fail on model. **Asaas** storefront use requires Venduá's CNPJ as parent of KYC'd sub-accounts. During the regulatory evaluation period that is capped at 10 sub-accounts per parent, with R$ 2.000 in charges per sub-account for the first period of up to 60 days ([Asaas subcontas](https://docs.asaas.com/docs/criacao-de-subcontas)). **Efí's** Pix split works only between Efí accounts and is configured by the platform. Split charges can't be refunded ([Efí Pix split](https://dev.efipay.com.br/en/docs/api-pix/split-de-pagamento-pix/)), which is a non-starter for a shop that must refund a cancelled delivery. Stripe fails on invite-only Pix ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/how-to-enable-pix-as-a-payment-method-in-brazil)). Whether Connect supports a Brazilian platform with Brazilian connected accounts was not verified: Stripe's cross-border payouts page doesn't list Brazil, but that page covers cross-border combinations only, and a weak search summary places Brazil on the Express connected-account list. Cielo, Rede and Getnet publish no online prices, and their connect flows weren't researched. The competitive context makes the zero-fee model a selling point. Food-ordering SaaS rivals bundle their own payments: Goomer charges 0,99% Pix at D+1 and 3,49% credit at D+30 ([Goomer help](https://ajuda.goomer.com.br/goomergo/painel/pagamento-online)), Anota AI 0–0,99% + R$ 0,50 per Pix ([Anota AI](https://anota.ai/blog/pagamento-online-para-restaurante/)) and Cardápio Web R$ 0,50 per Pix ([Cardápio Web](https://cardapioweb.com/blog/formas-de-pagamento-no-cardapio-digital/)). Mercado Pago at 0,99% matches them, and InfinitePay at 0% would beat them.

Two findings change the storefront plan. First, **PagBank is not the only other open one-click option: SumUp also qualifies**, pending SumUp's scope review and confirmation that Pix works for every Brazilian SumUp merchant. Second, **PagBank's Pix through Connect is still unproven**: a community post reports a 403 "whitelist required" on Pix QR in production, so a sandbox and production test with PagBank comes before any build.

**A plain Pix key is the zero-integration fallback.** Venduá can generate a static Pix QR (BR Code) from the key the merchant types, with the amount and a txid of up to 25 characters (BCB _Manual de Padrões para Iniciação do Pix_). The shopper's bank app shows the key holder's name and masked CPF/CNPJ before paying, which guards against a mistyped key. Venduá never learns that the payment happened, though, so the merchant confirms each order by hand. That is how Goomer's "Pix Manual" works. Automatic confirmation on a key Venduá doesn't control needs an Open Finance data-sharing consent (for example through Pluggy) or a regulated payment initiator, and both are heavier than a login. It suits a "pedir pelo WhatsApp"-style fallback, not the main checkout.

## Pix is nearly free everywhere; the card slice decides merchant cost

Payment mix explains why the fee spread matters less than it looks. Pix is now about **half of Brazilian e-commerce orders** (50,2% in Nuvemshop's May 2026 data), while boleto has collapsed to 0,9% ([Nuvemshop](https://www.nuvemshop.com.br/blog/pix-ecommerce/); [E-Commerce Brasil](https://www.ecommercebrasil.com.br/noticias/pix-ou-cartao-dados-mostram-como-o-brasileiro-paga-no-comercio-eletronico)). Credit cards carry a higher average ticket (R$ 340 vs R$ 234 for Pix). Retail stores with larger baskets therefore feel installment pricing, while R$ 40–120 food orders mostly don't. On a **R$ 60 Pix order**, the spread is R$ 0,00 (InfinitePay, Ton/Stone) to R$ 0,59 (MP, PagBank, Iugu) to R$ 0,71 (Efí, Stripe). Asaas's flat R$ 1,99 is the outlier and only beats a percentage fee on tickets above about R$ 200. On a **R$ 60 card à vista** the gap widens: about R$ 1,89 (InfinitePay D+1), R$ 1,91 (Ton/Stone), R$ 2,09 (Efí), R$ 2,28 (Asaas), R$ 2,52 (PagBank) and R$ 2,99 at Mercado Pago with immediate settlement, or R$ 2,39 if the merchant waits 30 days. For a Pix-heavy delivery shop, the provider choice is worth well under R$ 1 per card order. For a retail store selling R$ 300 baskets in 3x interest-free, it is worth much more. Applying MP's installment surcharge additively gives roughly **R$ 24–27 per R$ 300 sale** ([Marreira Digital](https://marreiradigital.com.br/calculadora-mercadopago/)). iDinheiro's MP table (2x 4,59%, 12x 17,28%) suggests the additive model overstates it ([iDinheiro](https://www.idinheiro.com.br/negocios/melhores-links-de-pagamento/)), so treat that range as an upper estimate.

Low installment rates hide a comparability trap. Asaas (3,49% + R$ 0,49 for 2–6x) and Efí (3,99%) look three times cheaper than Mercado Pago. But their headline rates appear to assume the merchant receives each parcel as it falls due. Advancing the money costs extra: Asaas lists 1,70% per month for anticipating parcelado receivables, and Efí 1,29% per anticipated parcel ([Asaas](https://www.asaas.com/precos-e-taxas); [Efí](https://sejaefi.com.br/tarifas)). By this report's own estimate, a 3x sale at Asaas fully anticipated lands near 7% (about R$ 21 on R$ 300). That is still cheaper than MP's immediate-settlement figure, but the gap is about one third, not two thirds. Fixed fees also bite at small tickets. Chargebacks are published only by Stripe, at R$ 55 per dispute ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/br/pricing)), and Asaas and Efí charge R$ 5 per TED withdrawal ([Asaas](https://www.asaas.com/precos-e-taxas); [Efí](https://sejaefi.com.br/tarifas)). Promotions such as PagBank's 0% for 30 days and Asaas's three-month discount are real but short-lived and revocable ([PagBank FAQ](https://faq.pagbank.com.br/duvida/quais-sao-as-taxas-para-vender-com-link-de-pagamento/1929)). They should never anchor a comparison or appear in Venduá's copy.

## Regulation rewards Venduá's no-custody design

The regulatory findings favour the current architecture, though they don't settle Venduá's legal classification. After the July 2025 attack on C&M Software (about R$ 800 million), BCB required prior authorization for every payment institution (IP), with a filing window of 1–31 May 2026 ([Agência Brasil](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/economia/noticia/2025-09/bc-endurece-regras-de-seguranca-para-instituicoes-de-pagamento)). It denied eight IP authorizations in January–April 2026 alone ([TJS Auditores](http://www.tjsauditores.com.br/2026/05/27/bc-ja-negou-autorizacao-para-oito-instituicoes-de-pagamento-em-2026/)). Res. BCB 522 (November 2025) pulled all subcredenciadores (sub-acquirers) into centralized settlement ([TIInside](https://tiinside.com.br/10/11/2025/bcb-aprimora-regulamentacao-de-arranjos-de-pagamento/); [BCB Voto 159/2025](https://normativos.bcb.gov.br/Votos/BCB/2025159/Voto_do_BC_159_2025.pdf)). The BaaS framework (Res. Conjunta 16, compliance by 31 December 2026) bans pooled "conta-bolsão" accounts. It also bars the client-facing company from receiving client funds in its own account ([NDM Advogados](https://ndmadvogados.com.br/artigo/guia-completo-banking-as-a-service-bcb/)). A platform whose shoppers pay straight into each merchant's own account at an authorized PSP avoids the custody and pooled-account problems these rules target. Not holding funds does not by itself rule out being a subcredenciador, which BCB defines by enabling merchants to accept payments, and no platform exemption text was found, so Venduá's classification needs a lawyer's opinion. That is the practical case against the sub-account models of Asaas, Efí and the split products: they move Venduá toward the roles BCB is tightening. Whether even a non-zero Mercado Pago `application_fee` changes Venduá's status wasn't settled by the sources. It needs a lawyer before the fee leaves zero. BCB is also designing a regulated "intermediaries in Pix" model for marketplaces. Consultations run in June and December 2026, with rules in 2027, and it is not yet in force ([BCB Fórum Pix](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/estabilidadefinanceira/pix/Forum_Pix_Plenaria/20260326-Forum_Pix.pdf)). The same rules make **PSP regulatory health a selection criterion**: prefer banks and large authorized IPs over small processors that depend on BaaS.

**MED 2.0 means an "approved" Pix is no longer final.** It became mandatory for all Pix participants on 2 February 2026 under Res. BCB 493 ([BCB Fórum Pix](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/estabilidadefinanceira/pix/Forum_Pix_Plenaria/20260326-Forum_Pix.pdf)). It traces funds through chained accounts, reportedly up to five layers deep. It can block them for up to 72 hours during analysis and is expected to refund within 11 days. It covers fraud, coercion and institution error, not buyer's remorse ([InfoMoney](https://www.infomoney.com.br/minhas-financas/med-2-0-passa-a-ser-obrigatorio-em-todas-as-plataformas-que-oferecem-pix/); [Transfeera](https://transfeera.com/blog/med-pix-2-0/)). Because funds land in the merchant's PSP account, blocks fall on the merchant, not Venduá. The kernel and admin should still handle post-approval refund or block webhooks from every provider, rather than treating Pix as irrevocable. Self-service MED in banking apps is planned for October 2026, which will make claims easier to file.

Several trends reshape what to expect from providers. **There is no BCB "Pix Parcelado"**: BCB dropped the plan in December 2025 and banned the name, so any installment-via-Pix offer is a PSP or BNPL credit product with its own contract and isn't portable across gateways ([Finsiders](https://finsidersbrasil.com.br/pagamentos/pix/exclusivo-bc-desiste-de-regulamentar-pix-parcelado-mas-veta-uso-do-nome/)). **Pix without redirect (JSR) via Open Finance** opened to the general market in April 2026, and the R$ 500 cap on it and on Pix por aproximação was lifted on 1 October 2026 ([Pluggy](https://www.pluggy.ai/blog/open-finance-2026-novidades); [ADVFN](https://br.advfn.com/jornal/2026/06/pix-por-aproximacao-tera-limite-flexivel-a-partir-de-outubro-banco-central-amplia-autonomia-dos-usuarios)). That makes higher-ticket Pix checkout smoother wherever a PSP exposes it. Two items are not yet in force: Res. BCB 587/2026 moves Pix Automático on salary accounts to 1 July 2027, and hybrid boleto+Pix to 1 February 2027 ([Cescon Barrieu](https://cesconbarrieu.com.br/resolucao-bcb-no-587-2026-novas-obrigacoes-e-impactos-para-participantes-do-pix/)). Debit and prepaid interchange have been capped at 0,5% and 0,7% since 2023, with no credit cap enacted ([Tecnoblog](https://tecnoblog.net/noticias/2022/09/27/bc-limita-tarifa-de-intercambio-de-cartoes-e-o-que-isso-muda/)).

**The tax reform touches Venduá's storefronts through liability, not split payment.** Split payment on consumer card and Pix sales has no schedule before 2028 ([Contábeis](https://www.contabeis.com.br/noticias/79382/split-payment-nao-atingira-vendas-a-pessoas-fisicas-e-cartoes-na-primeira-fase/)), so PSP split-readiness shouldn't drive the storefront choice. But LC 214/2025 makes a digital platform that controls an essential element of a sale (charging, payment, terms or delivery) jointly liable for IBS/CBS when the seller fails to issue the fiscal document. The liability is waived when split payment runs or the document is issued ([Pallotta Martins](https://pallottamartins.com.br/2025/10/29/lc-214-2025-plataformas-digitais-arrecadacao-tributaria/); [Systax](https://www.systax.com.br/artigos-e-novidades/como-a-reforma-tributaria-muda-a-responsabilidade-de-marketplaces-e-plataformas-digitais/)). Venduá's checkout plausibly controls such elements. Whether per-merchant storefront SaaS counts as a "platform" here is a legal question. The defensive product move is the same either way: make it easy for merchants to issue their fiscal document per order.

## Every number needs a provider quote before it reaches copy

This research rests on uneven ground, and the report's confidence should match it. **Official pages fetched**, though read through a summarising tool rather than verbatim, back the Asaas, Efí, Stripe, Vindi and PagBank-link figures. They also back InfinitePay's 0% Pix and the pagar.me landing-page numbers, though the latter two may be card-machine or promotional tables. **Secondary sources only** back every Mercado Pago rate (its pages returned 403), Ton/Stone, Iugu, Galax Pay, PicPay, and the Pagar.me figures from Chargefy, a competitor. **Unresolved conflicts** remain on six points. MP's 30-day card rate is 3,98–3,99% or 3,03%. MP's large-seller Pix discount appears as both "0,49%" and "R$ 0,49". PagBank's link card rate is 4,20% or 4,99% + R$ 0,40. Pagar.me's rates are a three-way conflict. Asaas's Pix is R$ 1,99 or R$ 0,49, and its card settlement is D+30/32 by its usual terms against a D+2–3 page summary that looks swapped with boleto. Efí's anticipation is 1,29% per parcel or 4% per bordereau capped at R$ 30.

Six questions must be answered by the provider, or by counsel, before any decision. Mercado Pago must give its subscription card rate on Venduá's account and say whether preapproval supports Pix Automático through the API. Asaas must confirm Pix Automático eligibility for Venduá's CNPJ and that it really costs the same as a normal Pix. PagBank must confirm that a Connect token can create Pix charges for the merchant without Split. InfinitePay must explain how to verify handle ownership, and its refund and sandbox support. Pagar.me must confirm that a no-split integration using the merchant's own keys is supported. Counsel must review Venduá's status under LC 214 and whether any non-zero application fee changes its BCB position. Under the repo's own rule, any public copy that states a rate, plan or price needs the user's explicit decision. That includes "Pix a partir de 0,99%" and "sem mensalidade".

## Conclusion

The research turns the gateway question from a price comparison into a liability comparison. The cheapest-looking options (Asaas, Efí, the split products) are cheap partly because the platform does the onboarding and carries the risk. Venduá's OAuth, no-custody design is both the cleanest regulatory position and a differentiator against food-ordering rivals that bundle payments with their own fees. Seen that way, the shortlist nearly writes itself: Mercado Pago, then PagBank (or SumUp, whichever passes a Pix test first), then InfinitePay's handle, with a plain Pix key as the no-integration fallback. Ton has no public API found. Stone's consent-based Open Bank flow (Pix `cob`/`cobv`, refunds, webhooks) and its Payment Links API need a Stone partnership, and neither gives a self-serve card-and-Pix integration. Pursuing a lower card rate through a sub-account model would trade a few tenths of a point for a compliance burden BCB is actively tightening.

For Venduá's own revenue, the largest available gain doesn't come from a better rate. It comes from turning the monthly manual Pix into a standing authorization. That makes a single email to Mercado Pago about Pix Automático the most valuable next step in this whole decision, worth more than any fee negotiation. MP's opacity is itself a finding: its pages couldn't be read, and its rates conflict across sources. Venduá should get a written rate card from every provider it depends on, starting with the incumbent.
